# Q1 Please show me examples of Deep learning, quantum computers, and generative AI have achieved the Turing test.
A1
The answer depends on what you mean by **"achieved the Turing test."**

The original Turing Test, proposed by Alan Turing, asks whether a human judge can reliably distinguish a machine from a human through conversation alone.

## Short Answer

| Technology        | Example                                                  | Widely Accepted as Passing?                             |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Deep Learning     | GPT-4, GPT-4.5, Llama 3.1-405B                           | Yes, according to several recent studies; still debated |
| Generative AI     | ChatGPT (GPT-4/4.5), Claude-class LLMs, Llama-class LLMs | Yes, strongest evidence so far                          |
| Quantum Computers | None                                                     | No known example                                        |

---

# 1. Deep Learning Examples

Deep learning is the technology underlying modern large language models.

### GPT-4

A 2024 controlled Turing-test study found that GPT-4 was judged human about 54% of the time in five-minute conversations. The authors described this as the first robust evidence that an artificial system passed an interactive Turing test. ([arXiv][1])

### GPT-4.5

A 2026 study reported that GPT-4.5 was mistaken for a human about 73% of the time under certain testing conditions, substantially exceeding chance levels. ([The Week][2])

### Llama 3.1-405B

The same 2026 study reported that Llama 3.1-405B was identified as human about 56% of the time. ([The Week][2])

# 2. Generative AI Examples

Generative AI is a broader category than deep learning, but today's successful generative AI systems are almost all based on deep neural networks.

Examples frequently cited as passing or nearly passing the Turing Test:

* ChatGPT (GPT-4, GPT-4.5)
* Claude
* Llama

The strongest published evidence currently comes from GPT-4-family systems. Researchers found that many participants could not distinguish GPT-4 from a human conversational partner. ([arXiv][1])

---

# 3. Historical Pre-Deep-Learning Example

### Eugene Goostman

Eugene Goostman was a rule-based chatbot that attracted attention in 2014 when organizers claimed it passed a version of the Turing Test.

However, many AI researchers criticized the result because:

* the test setup was limited,
* the chatbot used a fictional teenage persona,
* judges interacted only briefly.

Today most researchers consider GPT-4-level results more convincing than the Eugene Goostman demonstration. ([Lifewire][4])

---

# 4. Quantum Computer Examples

Currently:

**No quantum computer has passed the Turing Test.**

Examples of major quantum systems include:

* IBM quantum processors
* Google quantum processors
* D-Wave Systems
* IonQ

These machines are designed for:

* optimization,
* simulation,
* cryptography,
* quantum algorithms,

not natural-language conversation.

A quantum computer by itself does not chat with humans. To participate in a Turing Test, it would need an AI system running on top of it, and no quantum-AI conversational system has convincingly passed a Turing Test so far.

---

# Important Caveat

Many AI researchers argue that:

> Passing the Turing Test does **not** prove consciousness, understanding, or human-level intelligence.

It only shows that the system can convincingly imitate human conversation under the test conditions. This criticism has been discussed since Turing proposed the test in 1950 and remains active today. ([ACLアソシエーション][3])

## Bottom Line

* **Deep Learning:** GPT-4 and GPT-4.5 are the strongest examples and are often described as having passed modern Turing tests. ([arXiv][1])
* **Generative AI:** GPT-4-family systems are the leading examples. ([Live Science][5])
* **Quantum Computers:** No accepted example exists yet. Quantum computers are currently computation engines rather than conversational agents.
